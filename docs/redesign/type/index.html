Redesign · T1 typography · Liminal Sanctuary

Four type pairings, each shown at dusk and at dawn

Every pairing is set in the same two panels. Dusk is the twilight sky used for the landing, login and in-between moments. Dawn is the same sky at first light, used for the working screens. The panels keep their own colours whatever theme this page uses.

Judge the body text and the stress line first, and the hero line second. A patient reads the body for the whole session. The hero is read once.

All 4 pass the Catalan checkEvery face was checked in its font files for l·l (U+00B7), Ŀ ŀ (U+013F/0140), ç, à è é í ï ò ó ú ü, ñ, ¿ ¡ and « ».
Atkinson Hyperlegible is outBoth the original and Next lack Ŀ ŀ (U+013F/0140), so it fails the Catalan check. So do Ibarra Real Nova, Hedvig Letters, Sofia Sans and Red Hat Text.
Fraunces is goneIt was the board's first idea and the most recognisable “AI design” serif of 2024–26. See docs/redesign/06_TYPOGRAPHY.md for the reasoning.

Pairing 1 · recommended

1 Atrium: Castoro Titling + Castoro / Luciole

The liminal answer. Inscriptional Roman capitals by Tiro Typeworks (John Hudson), cut for titling rather than for “ancient” costume, with Castoro as the mixed-case partner. The body is Luciole, the one face here that was designed and tested with visually impaired readers. In the liminal atrium it reads as a name carved over a doorway, and the plain Luciole underneath says the room is for use, not for show.

  • OFL 1.1 (Castoro); CC BY 4.0 / OFL (Luciole)
  • Castoro: Google Fonts, @fontsource/castoro-titling, @fontsource/castoro
  • Luciole: not on npm, self-host the files
  • Castoro 400–700 + italic · Titling 400 · Luciole 400/700 + italics
  • Luciole x-height 0.545
  • Catalan ✓ checked in cmap

Pairing 2

2 Label: Spectral + Spectral SC / Inclusive Sans

The gallery wall. Spectral is Production Type’s open screen serif. It is sharp and quiet, and its small-caps cut makes exact museum labels. Inclusive Sans (Olivia King) is an open sans built around accessibility, with a serifed I, a tailed l and a slashed zero. In the liminal frame it reads as the wall text in an empty gallery after hours, more literary than monumental.

  • OFL 1.1 (both)
  • Google Fonts · @fontsource/spectral, @fontsource/spectral-sc, @fontsource-variable/inclusive-sans
  • Spectral 200–800 + italics (static) · Inclusive Sans 300–700 + italic
  • Inclusive Sans x-height 0.500
  • Catalan ✓ checked in cmap

Pairing 3

3 Signage: Tenor Sans / Readex Pro

The contemporary take, with no serifs. Tenor Sans has classical, wide proportions and works for wayfinding and station signs. Its spaced capitals evoke an atrium without quoting Rome. Readex Pro is the extended Lexend family (Thomas Jockin, Nadine Chahine and others), with a large x-height and an open, even rhythm. In the liminal frame it reads as a quiet transit hall or an arcade at closing time, the most “space” and least “museum” of the four.

  • OFL 1.1 (both)
  • Google Fonts · @fontsource/tenor-sans, @fontsource-variable/readex-pro
  • Tenor Sans 400 only · Readex Pro 160–700 + HEXP axis
  • Readex Pro x-height 0.525
  • Catalan ✓ checked in cmap

Pairing 4 · for contrast

4 Safe: Source Serif 4 / Source Sans 3

The control. Adobe’s open superfamily is documented, on every CDN and package registry, and nobody will object to it. It is here so the other three can be judged against something neutral. In the liminal frame it reads as a university press catalogue: competent, with no atmosphere. Look at its stress line: I and l are almost the same bar.

  • OFL 1.1 (both)
  • Google Fonts · @fontsource-variable/source-serif-4, @fontsource-variable/source-sans-3
  • Serif opsz 8–60 · wght 200–900 · Sans wght 200–900 · italics
  • Source Sans x-height 0.486
  • Catalan ✓ checked in cmap
